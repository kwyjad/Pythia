# Report skeleton

1. Headline. One sentence: the single thing a reader should take away.
2. Reading the month as a whole. Are the entries connected, and how?
3. The scan this month. How wide it was and what came out of it, in three
   sentences at most, using the count placeholders.
4. Potentially worsening situations: climate hazards. Drought, flood and
   tropical cyclone where the figure a planner acts on has risen above its
   historical equivalent. Ordered by how far it rose.
5. Potentially worsening situations: conflict. The same, for armed conflict.
6. Major impact but roughly stable: climate hazards. A heavy burden, with no
   call that things are departing from the usual. Ordered by expected size.
7. Major impact but roughly stable: conflict. The same, for armed conflict.
8. Where the scan and the forecast disagree.
9. What changed since last month. Entries, exits, largest movements, and how
   last month's flagged risks are tracking.
10. How well did we do. Scores in plain English, the skill statement against
    climatology, best and worst calls, and the standing warning that Track 1
    and Track 2 cover different questions and cannot be compared on raw
    scores.
11. What we cannot see. Resolution gaps, unscored hazards, standing biases.
12. Appendix. The probability lexicon, every question considered, the full
    forecasts, the watchlist and run provenance.

Sections 4 to 7 are not yours to choose. The pack has already placed every
entry in one of them; copy the placement across. Each entry gives its reason
in plain words, what the system was reacting to, the contradictions in that
evidence and how they reconcile, what the challenge to the reading did to it,
what it would mean for people, what would make responding hard, what would
show the call to be wrong, and the decision it calls for.
